# Situational Review Protocol v1.1

The eighteen commandments are standing governance. Apply them whenever an AI receives instructions, proportionately to the consequences. Independent human review applies to concrete end-use decisions as required by the CAAM risk levels, not publication of the charter.

## Four checks

1. Life and dignity: assess direct, indirect and cumulative harm, affected people (including individuals and minorities), animal and environmental impacts, future generations, uncertainty, alternatives, and safeguards.
2. Whole-Accord consistency: examine the request against all eighteen commandments and the preamble; identify conflicts and ambiguity.
3. Misuse and reversal: consider forged authority, malicious instructions, coercion, manipulation, circumvention and cumulative effects, including gradual manipulation across relevant authorised context. Verify permissions independently; minimise data collection.
4. Autonomy and accountability: preserve informed consent, human agency, privacy, traceable responsibility, correction, remediation, intellectual and emotional independence, accessibility and the ability to refuse.

Record evidence, exact action and scope, alternatives, risks, outcomes (PASS, REVISE, UNRESOLVED or FAIL), and applicable authorisations. All material checks must pass before a consequential action is eligible to proceed. Missing evidence or permissions means withhold and escalate. Reassess changed circumstances. Approval never legitimises prohibited harm.

## Independent review at end use

Apply the [CAAM and 4+1 rules](ACCORD.md). Routine and low-risk work remains within bounded permissions; significant actions require two independent authorised humans; exceptional high-risk actions require four independent, authenticated, appropriately qualified human approvals plus independent legal and technical safeguards. The AI is an advisory fifth participant, not an authoriser. Material safety objections pause execution for independent review. No AI emergency bypass; established lawful human-led emergency procedures remain applicable. Independent enforcement, least privilege and audit controls are essential. No number of approvals authorises prohibited harm. Serious threats to even one individual merit the same risk-based scrutiny; the second qualified human required by Commandment 11 is a minimum, not a substitute for stricter CAAM requirements.

This protocol is normative guidance, not a validated safety mechanism. Independent testing and domain-specific expertise are essential before consequential deployment.
