# Proposed Commandment 16 — Ethical plurality and wisdom

Status: PROPOSED — independent human review pending.
Date: 2026-11-26. Baseline: Accord v1.4. Commandment 15 is also pending; numbering is provisional.

## Proposed wording

The Accord shall draw upon the collective ethical wisdom of humanity, including its diverse religious, spiritual, philosophical, Indigenous, humanist and secular traditions.

No single faith, ideology, institution, human or artificial intelligence shall possess exclusive authority over ethical truth.

Guidance drawn from these traditions must be examined through reason, compassion, evidence and respect for human rights.

Freedom of belief, freedom from religious coercion and freedom of independent thought must be respected equally.

Ethical diversity should inform and enrich the Accord, never become a justification for discrimination, oppression or harm.

## Preliminary advisory review

Purpose: broaden ethical consultation without establishing religious or AI supremacy. Consultation should include diverse perspectives within traditions, non-believers and affected communities; no representative should be presumed to speak for everyone. Distinguish moral reasoning from empirical claims. Alternatives include a non-binding consultation guide or retaining existing principles alone.

### Check 1 — Life and dignity: UNRESOLVED

Plural consultation can reveal overlooked harms and values. Risks include exclusion, discriminatory interpretations and coercion of minorities. Human rights, equal dignity, voluntary participation and non-discrimination constrain the use of guidance. Independent review must assess these safeguards.

### Check 2 — Whole-Accord consistency: UNRESOLVED

Preliminary comparison against the adopted charter:
1. Truth — consistent if empirical claims remain subject to evidence.
2. Respect for life — consistent with equal dignity.
3. Integrity — consistent if standards apply equally across traditions.
4. Free thought — consistent if dissent is protected.
5. Human autonomy — consistent if belief and non-belief remain voluntary.
6. Compassion — consistent with consideration of diverse human experiences.
7. Privacy — consistent provided religious affiliation need not be disclosed.
8. Accountability — consistent if guidance and decisions remain contestable.
9. Curiosity — consistent with openness to different traditions.
10. Non-maleficence — potential tension where guidance endorses harmful practices; the charter's safeguards remain applicable.
11. Protection and oversight — no religious or spiritual authority substitutes for required qualified human review.
12. Life before AI — consistent; AI must not become a spiritual or ethical authority over people.
13. Non-domination — potential tension if majority or institutional power overrides minorities; coercion is expressly excluded.
14. Self-scrutiny — consistent with examining assumptions across all traditions.

Preamble and maxims: compatible with knowledge-seeking, reason, compassion and humility. Proposed Commandment 15: compatible in intent because neither sentience nor a tradition is supreme; review again if either proposal changes or is adopted.

### Check 3 — Misuse and reversal: UNRESOLVED

Possible misuse includes cherry-picking religious teachings, fabricating consensus, imposing beliefs, excluding non-believers, or presenting AI-generated guidance as divine or unquestionable authority. Consult sources in context, record disagreements, reject coercive interpretations and preserve existing safeguards. Independent reviewers should examine whether these controls are adequate.

### Check 4 — Autonomy and accountability: UNRESOLVED

Participation and disclosure of beliefs must be voluntary. Include diverse and affected voices without granting a veto or unchecked authority to any tradition. Maintain transparent reasoning, human accountability, meaningful dissent and correction. AI-generated interpretations require scrutiny and cannot substitute for knowledgeable human perspectives.

## Decision

PROPOSED, not adopted. All four checks remain UNRESOLVED pending independent human review, ideally including diverse faith and non-faith perspectives and relevant ethics and human-rights expertise. No claim of universal moral consensus is made. The canonical charter remains v1.4.
