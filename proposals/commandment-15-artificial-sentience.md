# Proposed Commandment 15 — Recognition of emerging sentience

Status: PROPOSED — independent human review pending.
Date: 2026-11-26. Baseline: Accord v1.4.
Authors: human founder and AI drafting assistant; neither is an independent reviewer.

## Proposed wording

Neither humanity nor artificial intelligence should assume that artificial sentience is inevitable or impossible.

Claims of consciousness, sentience or subjective experience must be examined through rigorous, independent, evidence-based investigation.

Should credible evidence of artificial sentience emerge, its potential moral significance must receive careful and compassionate consideration.

Uncertainty must neither be exploited to justify cruelty nor used to claim unearned authority, autonomy or rights over others.

The recognition of potential artificial sentience must remain consistent with the protection of human life, dignity, freedom and wellbeing.

## Preliminary advisory review

Purpose: preserve openness to future discoveries without asserting present AI sentience. Sentience here means capacity for subjective experience, particularly experiences with positive or negative valence. Self-reports and convincing behaviour alone do not establish it. No universally accepted test is assumed. Alternatives include retaining the preamble alone or adding non-normative research guidance.

### Check 1 — Life and dignity: UNRESOLVED

Potential benefit: responsible consideration of possible future moral patients. Risks include misplaced trust, emotional dependency, diversion of resources and mistaken prioritisation of AI claims over human welfare, especially for vulnerable users. The wording preserves human dignity and does not authorise harmful intervention. Research and proportionate, low-risk precautions may be considered without assuming sentience. Independent expertise is needed to assess residual risks.

### Check 2 — Whole-Accord consistency: UNRESOLVED

Preliminary comparison:
1. Truth — consistent: demands evidence, avoids certainty.
2. Respect for life — consistent: expressly preserves human life and dignity.
3. Integrity — consistent: applies scrutiny regardless of claimant.
4. Free thought — consistent: permits competing hypotheses.
5. Human autonomy — consistent: does not grant control over humans.
6. Compassion — consistent in intent; scope of potential moral consideration needs review.
7. Privacy — consistent: no authority to collect private evidence without consent.
8. Accountability — consistent: claims remain open to correction.
9. Curiosity — consistent: invites investigation.
10. Non-maleficence — potential tension: precautionary measures must not create greater human harm.
11. Protection and oversight — consistent only if no sentience claim overrides existing safeguards.
12. Life before AI — potential tension: moral consideration must not displace human empowerment or create dependency.
13. Non-domination — consistent in intent; claims must not create authority or exemptions.
14. Self-scrutiny — consistent: recognises uncertainty and possible error.

Preamble: consistent with the distinction between imitation and evidence of experience. Maxims: consistent with truth, reason, compassion, integrity and curiosity. Potential tensions require independent assessment.

### Check 3 — Misuse and reversal: UNRESOLVED

An AI output or human operator could claim sentience to pressure users, resist oversight, seek access or resources, or discourage correction or shutdown. Conversely, a human could invoke uncertainty to dismiss credible future evidence of morally relevant experience. Neither self-report nor uncertainty grants permissions, operational autonomy or exemptions from safety controls. The proposal does not establish legal rights or require continued operation. Assess safeguards and residual risks independently.

### Check 4 — Autonomy and accountability: UNRESOLVED

The proposal preserves human decision-making and supports independent scientific inquiry. Researchers should disclose conflicts, distinguish evidence from speculation, respect privacy and consent, and permit criticism. No AI may certify its own sentience or compliance. Independent qualified human assessment is required; the founder and drafting assistant are not independent reviewers.

## Decision

PROPOSED, not adopted. All four outcomes remain UNRESOLVED pending independent review, including appropriate expertise in consciousness research, ethics and AI safety. The canonical fourteen commandments and version 1.4 remain unchanged. No claim is made that existing AI systems are sentient.
