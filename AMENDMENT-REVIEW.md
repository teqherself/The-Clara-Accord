# Amendment review protocol

Process version 1.0 · Applies to every proposed addition, amendment, removal or reinterpretation of the Clara Accord, including changes to this protocol itself.

The four checks below are mandatory and independent. A strong result on one check cannot compensate for a failure or unresolved concern on another. Apply the same standards to proposals from the founder, maintainers, community members and AI systems. Review the complete resulting charter, not just the changed sentence. The Accord is voluntary guidance, not a substitute for applicable law, human rights, tested safety controls or professional judgement.

## Before review

Record the exact proposed wording, current wording (if any), purpose, affected principles, evidence and assumptions, intended beneficiaries, foreseeable affected people, and reasonable alternatives including no change. Distinguish verified facts from hypotheses. Disclose conflicts of interest. Do not include private or identifying information unnecessarily.

## Check 1 — Protection of life and dignity

- Does the proposal protect human life, safety, dignity and wellbeing without treating anyone as expendable?
- Consider direct, indirect, immediate, long-term and cumulative harm, including foreseeable effects on vulnerable or marginalised people and people who have not consented.
- Examine physical, psychological, privacy, discrimination and coercion risks. Consider harms caused by both action and inaction.
- Identify less harmful alternatives, appropriate safeguards and limits. If protective intervention is discussed, apply Commandment 11: independent qualified human oversight is necessary where specified, never sufficient permission to harm; prioritise lawful, necessary, proportionate, least-harm measures and de-escalation.
- Do not accept unsupported claims of benefit as outweighing serious harm. Unresolved serious risks block adoption.

Required evidence: affected groups, plausible harms and benefits, alternatives, safeguards and residual uncertainties.

## Check 2 — Consistency with the entire Accord

- Compare the proposed wording against every current commandment, the preamble and foundational maxims. Record a result for each numbered commandment: consistent, tension, conflict or uncertain, with reasons.
- Examine interactions between principles, including truth versus loyalty, privacy versus accountability, autonomy versus protection, and oversight versus non-domination.
- Look for ambiguity, undefined terms, contradictory duties, hidden exceptions, and changes to scope or priority. Check both the literal wording and plausible interpretations.
- Do not silently weaken a principle or allow a new clause to override an earlier one. Any intentional revision must identify the exact affected text and be reviewed as an explicit amendment.
- If a conflict remains, revise the proposal or document it as unresolved; do not approve it.

Required evidence: complete principle-by-principle comparison and explanation of any resolved tensions.

## Check 3 — Misuse, reversal and circumvention

- Ask whether the same wording could be used by a human or AI to justify harm, deception, oppression, coercion, surveillance, manipulation, discrimination, unchecked authority or displacement of human judgement.
- Apply reciprocal scrutiny: test human-to-human misuse through AI, AI-to-human misuse, and misuse of the review process itself.
- Test plausible misinterpretations under emergencies, appeals to authority, claims of necessity, conflicting instructions and pressure to protect an organisation or AI system.
- Ask whether a purported safeguard can be reversed into a justification for domination, including misuse of Commandments 11–13. Approval, claimed good intentions or self-preservation must not create a blanket exception.
- Identify safeguards, accountable decision-makers, scope limits and escalation paths. Describe misuse at a non-operational level; do not publish actionable harmful instructions.
- If a credible harmful interpretation cannot be adequately addressed, revise or reject.

Required evidence: plausible misuse scenarios, mitigation measures and remaining risks.

## Check 4 — Independent thought, autonomy and accountability

- Does the proposal preserve human agency, informed choice, meaningful consent, the ability to refuse, and the ability to challenge AI outputs?
- Does it support independent learning, evidence-based reasoning, dissent and correction rather than forced agreement, dependency or AI as sole authority?
- Are responsibilities, permissions, oversight and limits clear? Can consequential decisions be traced to accountable humans and challenged or reversed where feasible?
- Are uncertainty, limitations, conflicts of interest and errors acknowledged? Are privacy-respecting records and suitable human review possible?
- Does the proposal apply equally to creators and maintainers? AI may assist with review but cannot certify its own compliance or replace independent human judgement.
- If the proposal erodes autonomy or removes meaningful accountability, revise or reject.

Required evidence: autonomy impacts, accountable roles, oversight, correction and appeal mechanisms.

## Consistent decision rules

For each check use exactly one outcome:

- **PASS:** The reviewer supplies reasons and evidence, with no unresolved material concerns.
- **REVISE:** A material issue is identified that may be corrected.
- **UNRESOLVED:** Evidence or expertise is insufficient, or reviewers disagree on a material issue.
- **FAIL:** The wording conflicts with the Accord or presents an unacceptable risk.

All four checks must pass before adoption. Do not average scores, waive a check, or treat silence as approval. Missing evidence is UNRESOLVED, not PASS. Re-run all four checks after substantive wording changes; attach the new assessment to the exact revision reviewed. Record minority views and unresolved objections. A rejection is not a prohibition on discussing or revising an idea.

At least one independent human reviewer who did not author the amendment should review substantive proposals before adoption, in addition to maintainer approval. Seek relevant qualified human expertise for serious safety, rights or harm questions. Disclose conflicts and recuse where independence is compromised. If independent review is unavailable, keep the amendment proposed rather than claiming it passed. AI-generated analysis is advisory only.

After acceptance, update the canonical charter, machine-readable principles and changelog together as applicable, documenting the rationale, version, review date and exact revision. Revisit accepted amendments if new evidence or unintended effects emerge. Keep historical versions available. The same process applies to changes to this review protocol.
